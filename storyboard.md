# Storyboard: *A Day in the Stone Age*

**Format:** 2D cartoon short · **Target length:** ~90 seconds · **Audience:** Children and families

## Character and visual guide

- **Nia:** A curious child with a simple woven tunic, a small carry bag, and a bright expression. Keep her design consistent in every shot.
- **Nia's family and neighbors:** A diverse, cooperative group. Clothing and tools are simple, practical, and appropriate to the fictional setting.
- **Art direction:** Hand-drawn storybook look; rounded shapes, warm ochre and terracotta, greens and sky blues; lively faces; gentle camera moves.
- **Continuity:** This is an imaginative, general introduction. Stone Age lifeways differed by region and period.

## Narration only

> Long, long ago, before cities and cars, people lived close to the land. Nia’s community worked together. They made useful tools by shaping stone, wood, and other materials. Some gathered plants and berries. Others fished or hunted. What people ate depended on the place and season. People learned to use fire for warmth, light, and cooking. Around it, everyone could share a meal. Nia helps carry food home. Looking after one another made the whole community stronger. People also made art. Pictures and marks on stone show that they observed the world and shared ideas. They learned from experience and passed knowledge from one generation to the next. The Stone Age lasted a very long time. People everywhere found clever ways to live. What would you like to discover?

## Reusable visual prompt

> Create a single frame for a warm, child-friendly 2D storybook cartoon about a fictional Stone Age community. Hand-drawn rounded shapes, expressive faces, earthy ochre and terracotta with green and sky-blue accents, gentle natural lighting, respectful portrayal of skilled people cooperating. Keep character designs consistent with the storyboard. No text, no modern objects, no photorealism, no frightening violence.

For consistency, add the specific shot description and character details to the prompt for each frame. Review historical details before presenting the video as factual.
